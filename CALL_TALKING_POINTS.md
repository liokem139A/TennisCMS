## Discovery Call Framework (30 Minutes)

**Goal**: Understand their business, goals, and priorities → Identify if partnership fit exists → Build relationship for next conversation

**Structure**:
1. **Opening** (2 min): Welcome, thank you, agenda setting
2. **Community Intro** (3 min): Who we are, what we do, why relevant to them
3. **Their Business** (8 min): Ask questions, listen, understand goals
4. **Sponsorship/Partnership Exploration** (10 min): Explore potential fit, packages, activation
5. **Next Steps** (4 min): Clarify timing, next conversation, close call
6. **Buffer**: 3 min for overflow

**Tone**: Consultative, curious, respectful of their time. NOT a sales pitch.

---

## OPENING (2 Minutes)

### Call Start Script

```
"Hi [Name], thanks so much for making time today! I really appreciate it.

Quick agenda: I just want to learn more about [Company] and your sponsorship/
partnership goals. Then I'll share a bit about [Community] and we can explore 
if there's a natural fit.

Does that sound good? And just to confirm - do you have about 30 minutes?"

[Pause for confirmation]
```

### Key Opening Points:
- Personalize: Use their name, reference something about their company
- Respect time: Confirm they have 30 minutes
- Set expectations: Be clear this is exploratory, not a hard sell
- Listen: 70% listening, 30% talking on the call

## COMMUNITY INTRO (3 Minutes)

### Script Template (Customize for Prospect Type)

```
"So here's a quick background on [Community]: We're a [description: 
grassroots tennis community / wellness platform / athletic community] 
focused on [key mission: player development, community engagement, 
wellness, etc.].

We have [X thousand] active members/participants who [describe: compete 
in tournaments, use our platform, engage in events, etc.]. Our community is 
really passionate about [key value: health, competition, community, etc.].

Why I thought of [Company]: We follow what you're doing around [specific 
sponsorship/initiative], and I think there's a genuine alignment with 
[shared value: grassroots engagement, wellness focus, community building, etc.].

Does that make sense?"
```

### Customization by Prospect Type:

**For Corporate/National Brands** (Wilson, Lululemon, Liquid IV):
- Emphasize: Scale, member engagement, proven tournament/event infrastructure
- Key stat: "8,000+ active members" or "[X] annual events" or "reach to [demographic]"
- Reference their sponsorship model: "We've seen you sponsor [similar event/community]"

**For Tech Platforms** (SwingVision, Peloton):
- Emphasize: Community scale, user engagement, data/content opportunity
- Key stat: "Daily active users reaching [segment]" or "content generation"
- Reference: "We think co-branded content around [topic] could be valuable"

**For Local Businesses** (Tennis Club, Restaurant, Wealth Manager):
- Emphasize: Local community, member cross-promotion, mutual benefit
- Key stat: "Active community members in [your neighborhood]"
- Reference: "We'd love to work with local businesses we believe in"

## THEIR BUSINESS (8 Minutes)

### Key Questions to Ask (Pick 4-5 most relevant)

```
1. "Tell me about your current sponsorship strategy. What are you looking 
   to achieve with sponsorships right now?"

2. "What kind of community or audience would be ideal for [Company]?"

3. "Are there specific activations or partnership models that work best for you?"
   [Listen for: Event presence, content, digital, sampling, etc.]

4. "What does success look like for a sponsorship partnership?"
   [Listen for: Audience reach, brand visibility, ROI metrics, engagement]

5. "What's your timeline looking like for new partnerships in 2026?"
   [Listen for: Budget availability, decision timeline, process]

6. "Are there any current partnerships or initiatives you're excited about?"
   [Understand what's working for them]

7. "What are the biggest challenges you're facing right now?"
   [Opportunity to position how we can help]
```

### Active Listening Tactics:
- Let them talk (they'll tell you what matters most)
- Take notes (shows you care, helps you remember)
- Ask follow-up questions ("Can you tell me more about that?")
- Reflect back: "So it sounds like [goal] is really important...")
- Avoid: Interrupting, pitching too early, talking about yourself too much

### Red Flags to Note:
- "We don't do sponsorships right now" → Opportunity to re-frame as partnership
- "We're all set with partners" → Ask when they'll be reviewing next year
- "Our budget is tiny" → Not necessarily a deal-breaker; smaller prospects can be high-engagement
- "I'm not the decision-maker" → Get introduced to actual decision-maker; still valuable intel

### Green Flags (Signs of Real Interest):
- Asks specific questions about community/audience
- Discusses their business goals unprompted
- Mentions budget or timeline
- Asks what sponsorship/partnership would look like
- References similar partnerships they've done

## PARTNERSHIP EXPLORATION (10 Minutes)

### Pitch Opening (Transition from Listening)

```
"This is really helpful. Based on what you're saying, I think there could be 
a real opportunity here.

[Company], we're thinking about [specific partnership/sponsorship opportunity 
tailored to them]. Here's what I'm imagining..."
```

### Concept Pitches (Choose 1-2 Most Relevant)

**TECH PLATFORM PARTNERSHIPS** (SwingVision, Peloton):

```
"What if we created co-branded [content/features] that served both audiences?

For example: SwingVision - we could offer free video analysis to our top players, 
and you'd get incredible player adoption and content (all those videos, testimonials, 
content they'd generate).

Peloton - we could create a tennis-focused fitness series with your trainers, 
and cross-promote to each other's communities. Your members get new content, 
ours get exclusive classes.

Does that kind of partnership model resonate with you?"
```

**EQUIPMENT/BEVERAGE SPONSORSHIPS** (Wilson, Liquid IV):

```
"I'm thinking two paths here:

One: Tournament/Event Sponsorship - where you're the official [sponsor type] 
for our [tournament/event]. We get equipment/product, you get brand visibility, 
content, and athlete testimonials.

Two: Community Program - where we integrate your product into our member experience 
year-round. You'd get grassroots engagement and player feedback.

Which of these feels more aligned with what you're looking for?"
```

**LOCAL PARTNERSHIPS** (Tennis Club, Restaurant):

```
"Here's what I'm thinking: We could do a real collaboration where we cross-promote 
to each other's communities.

For you: You'd get visibility with [X hundred] engaged community members who are 
interested in [your offering]. Maybe a joint event or exclusive member benefit.

For us: We get to offer our community something cool and build that local partnership vibe.

Could we pilot something like this and see if it works for both of us?"
```

**CSR/HEALTH PARTNERSHIPS** (CVS/Aetna):

```
"Given your focus on community health, I'm thinking about a deeper partnership 
around [specific health/wellness initiative].

For example: What if we co-developed a community wellness program that could 
serve your members and the broader community? You'd get:
- Community health impact
- Member engagement/retention angle
- Content and testimonials
- Data on health/wellness outcomes

What would be most valuable for you?"
```

### Questions to Gauge Interest:

```
"What's your gut reaction to that idea?"

"What would you need to see to move forward with something like this?"

"Are there any concerns or objections I should know about?"

"If the fit looked good, what would the next step be on your end?"
```

### Discovery Questions (Listen for These):

- **Budget Clarity**: "What budget range are we talking about?"
- **Timeline**: "When would you need to make a decision?"
- **Activation Preference**: "What type of partnership works best for your team?"
- **Metrics**: "How would we measure success?"
- **Decision Process**: "Who else needs to be involved in the decision?"

### If They Say "We're Interested":

```
"That's great! Here's what I'm thinking for next steps:

1. I'll send you some more detailed information about [Community] and what 
   different partnership levels could look like

2. We can schedule a follow-up call for [specific timeframe] to dive deeper

3. If it still feels right, we can start exploring terms and next steps

Does that timeline work for you?"
```

### If They Say "Not Right Now" or "Let's Talk Later":

```
"I totally understand. Can I ask - what would need to change for this to 
be interesting?

[Listen]

Got it. Here's what I'll do: I'll stay in touch and circle back in [specific 
month/quarter]. In the meantime, if anything changes, feel free to reach out."

[Get their best contact info and note in tracker]
```

## CLOSING (4 Minutes)

### Next Steps Conversation

Always, ALWAYS end with clear next steps:

```
"So here's what we'll do:

1. [Action item 1 - usually your action]: I'll send you [materials/info]

2. [Action item 2 - their action]: You'll [review/think about/check with team]

3. [Next meeting]: We'll plan to touch base on [specific date/week]

Does that work?"

[Confirm date/email]
```

### Getting Intro to Decision-Maker (If Needed)

```
"I appreciate your time. Just to confirm - are you the right person to move 
this forward, or should I also connect with [title/person]?"

[If no]: "Would you be open to introducing us? I'm happy to send along 
any materials first."
```

### Thank You + Follow-Up

```
"Thanks so much for your time today. I really enjoyed learning about 
[Company] and your priorities.

I'll get [promised materials] over to you by [specific date], and we can 
connect again [specific timeframe].

Great talking with you!"
```

## DISCOVERY CALL SCORING (After the Call)

After each call, score the prospect on:

### Fit Score (1-5 stars)
- 5 stars: Perfect alignment, high interest, clear partnership opportunity
- 4 stars: Strong fit, genuine interest, some exploration needed
- 3 stars: Moderate fit, interested but need to explore more
- 2 stars: Weak fit, not enthusiastic, long shot
- 1 star: No fit, no interest, unlikely to pursue

### Likelihood (1-5 scale)
- 5: Will move forward with partnership
- 4: Very likely if proposal looks good
- 3: Possible if package fits
- 2: Unlikely, but open to exploring
- 1: Probably won't happen

### Budget Confidence (1-5 scale)
- 5: Budget confirmed, clear range
- 4: Likely has budget in our range
- 3: Possible but uncertain
- 2: Doubtful about budget availability
- 1: Unlikely to have budget

### Category (for Phase 2 prioritization)
- 🔴 HOT: Prioritize immediately for Phase 2 pitch
- 🟡 WARM: Develop further, consider for Phase 2
- 🟢 COOL: Monitor, might revisit next quarter

## COMMON OBJECTIONS & RESPONSES

**Objection 1: "We don't have budget for sponsorships"**

Response:
```
"I totally understand budget is tight. That's actually why I wanted to talk - 
we might be thinking about this differently.

Rather than a cash sponsorship, what if we explored a [barter/trade/co-marketing] 
model where we both get value without a big cash outlay?

For example: [specific idea]. Could something like that work?"
```

**Objection 2: "We're all set with partnerships"**

Response:
```
"Totally get it - you probably have good partners right now. I'm not trying 
to replace anyone.

I'm just curious: when you review your partnership portfolio [annually/next quarter], 
would you be open to exploring if there are any gaps we could fill or areas 
where we could add unique value?"
```

**Objection 3: "Our budget is really small"**

Response:
```
"No problem at all. Actually, some of our best partnerships start small and 
grow over time.

Here's what I'm thinking: Could we pilot something at a smaller scale to test 
if the partnership works, and then expand if we're both happy?

For example: [specific pilot idea for their business level]"
```

**Objection 4: "I'm not the decision-maker"**

Response:
```
"No problem - thanks for being upfront about that. 

Would you be open to introducing me to [person/title] so I can share this idea 
with them directly? I can also send over a quick summary first if that's easier.

Either way, I really appreciate your feedback today."
```

**Objection 5: "We need more information before we can decide"**

Response:
```
"Absolutely. Here's what I'll send you [specific materials]:
- [Info doc about community]
- [Sponsorship packages]
- [ROI examples]

After you've had a chance to review, when could we schedule a follow-up call? 
I'm happy to answer any questions you have."
```
