## DISCOVERY CALL FRAMEWORK (Quick Reference)

**Call Duration**: 30-45 minutes  
**Objective**: Understand sponsorship priorities + validate partnership fit  

### Call Structure
1. **Opening** (5 min): Build rapport, set agenda ("I'm in discovery mode")
2. **Discovery** (20-25 min): Ask about sponsorship goals, pain points, success metrics
3. **Pitch** (10 min): Explain partnership model + value
4. **Closing** (5-10 min): Next steps, timeline, how to move forward

### Key Discovery Questions
1. "What does sponsorship success look like for [Company] in 2026-2027?"
2. "What's your typical decision timeline for partnerships?"
3. "What would make this a no-brainer for your team?"
4. "What budget range are you comfortable discussing?"
5. "What's the next step in your partnership evaluation process?"

### Objection Handling (Sample Responses)

**Objection 1**: "We don't have budget for new sponsorships"  
**Response**: "Totally understandable. Many companies are re-evaluating their sponsorship mix. Our question is: IF you were going to allocate budget, what would success look like? We may not be fit for 2026, but we could be for 2027."

**Objection 2**: "This feels like a pitch, but I'm not ready to talk sponsorship"  
**Response**: "I appreciate that — that's exactly why I called. I'm NOT pitching you today. I'm actually trying to understand whether there's even a conversation to have. Based on what you've shared, it sounds like timing might not work. Would it be helpful if I circled back in Q4 when you're planning 2027?"

**Objection 3**: "We only sponsor major tours / established teams, not platforms"  
**Response**: "That makes sense. We're different — we're building community infrastructure, not competing with tours. Think of us more like a gym or studio that attracts members. Many sponsors partner at our level while also sponsoring tours. Does that change how you think about it?"

**Objection 4**: "This is interesting, but we need board approval"  
**Response**: "Totally understandable. What's the timeline and process for that approval? Can you help us understand who we need to talk to, or should we move this forward when you have more clarity?"
